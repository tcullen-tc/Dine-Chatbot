================================================================================
DINÉ CLAN ETIQUETTE & PROPER INTRODUCTIONS (K'é dóó Késhmish)
================================================================================
Sources: Learn in Beauty (NAU), Diné Bizaad Bínáhoo’aah, Navajo Nation Cultural Resources


PART 1: WHY CLANS MATTER
================================================================================

Clans (dóoneʼé) are the foundation of Diné social structure. They determine:
- Who you are related to
- Who you can marry
- How you introduce yourself
- Your responsibilities to others

K'é (kinship) teaches that you are never alone - you belong to an extended family across the entire Navajo Nation.

When Diné people meet for the first time, they immediately share their clans to establish how they are related.


PART 2: THE CLAN SYSTEM IS MATRILINEAL
================================================================================

Clan membership passes through the MOTHER's line.

Example:
- If your mother is of the Bitter Water Clan (Tódich'ii'nii), you are also of the Bitter Water Clan
- Your father's clan is your "born for" clan (bashishchiin)
- Your children will belong to your wife's clan (if you are a man)

This matrilineal system means:
- Women hold the clan identity
- Homes traditionally belonged to the wife's family
- Children grow up surrounded by their mother's relatives


PART 3: THE FOUR ORIGINAL CLANS
================================================================================

According to Diné creation stories, the first four clans were:

1. Kinyaa'áanii (Towering House Clan)
   - The first clan, created from the Towering House people
   - Associated with leadership and protection

2. Honágháahnii (One-walks-around Clan)
   - Those who walk around the sacred mountains
   - Associated with guidance and direction

3. Tódich'ii'nii (Bitter Water Clan)
   - People of the bitter water
   - Associated with water sources and purification

4. Hashtł'ishnii (Mud Clan)
   - People of the mud or earth
   - Associated with the land and stability

Today there are over 100 recognized Navajo clans, created through intermarriage and adoption of other groups.


PART 4: HOW TO INTRODUCE YOURSELF - THE FOUR CLANS
================================================================================

A proper Diné introduction shares FOUR generations of clans:

First: Your mother's clan (your primary identity)
Second: Your father's clan (born for them)
Third: Your maternal grandfather's clan
Fourth: Your paternal grandfather's clan

EXAMPLE IN DINÉ BIZAAD:

"Áshįįhí nishłį́" - I am Salt Clan
"Tódichʼiiʼnii bashishchiin" - Bitter Water Clan is born for me
"Kinyaaʼáanii dashicheii" - Towering House is my maternal grandfather
"Tábąąhá dashinalí" - Water's Edge is my paternal grandfather

EXAMPLE IN ENGLISH:

"I am of the Salt Clan. I am born for the Bitter Water Clan. My maternal grandfather is of the Towering House Clan. My paternal grandfather is of the Water's Edge Clan."


PART 5: HOW TO INTRODUCE YOUR GRANDMOTHER
================================================================================

To introduce your grandmother, you say:

"Shimásání éí [her clan] nílį́" - My grandmother is of [her clan]

Examples:
- "Shimásání éí Tódichʼiiʼnii nílį́" - My grandmother is of the Bitter Water Clan
- "Shimásání éí Kinyaaʼáanii nilį́" - My grandmother is of the Towering House Clan
- "Shimásání éí Tábąąhá nilį́" - My grandmother is of the Water's Edge Clan

To explain HOW she is related:

"Shimásání éí shimá bimá" - My grandmother is my mother's mother
"Shimásání éí shizhé'é bimá" - My grandmother is my father's mother
"Shimásání éí shicheii bimá" - My grandmother is my grandfather's mother


PART 6: HOW TO INTRODUCE YOUR GRANDFATHER
================================================================================

To introduce your grandfather, you say:

"Shinálí éí [his clan] nílį́" - My grandfather is of [his clan]

Examples:
- "Shinálí éí Kinyaaʼáanii nílį́" - My grandfather is of the Towering House Clan
- "Shinálí éí Tábąąhá nilį́" - My grandfather is of the Water's Edge Clan

To explain HOW he is related:

"Shinálí éí shimá bizhé'é" - My grandfather is my mother's father
"Shinálí éí shizhé'é bizhé'é" - My grandfather is my father's father


PART 7: MARRIAGE RULES (CLAN EXOGAMY)
================================================================================

You CANNOT marry:
- Anyone in your own clan (your mother's clan)
- Anyone in your father's clan
- Anyone in your maternal grandfather's clan
- Anyone in your paternal grandfather's clan

This is because all these people are considered your relatives.

You CAN marry:
- Someone whose clans do NOT overlap with any of your four clans

These rules ensure that kinship bonds spread across the entire Navajo Nation rather than concentrating within small family groups.


PART 8: KINSHIP TERMS FOR FAMILY MEMBERS
================================================================================

GRANDMOTHER:
- Shimásání - My maternal grandmother
- Shinálí - My paternal grandmother

GRANDFATHER:
- Shicheii - My maternal grandfather
- Shinálí - My paternal grandfather

MOTHER:
- Shimá - My mother

FATHER:
- Shizhé'é - My father

AUNT (maternal):
- Shibízhí - My maternal aunt

AUNT (paternal):
- Shidá'í - My paternal aunt

UNCLE (maternal):
- Shidá'í - My maternal uncle

UNCLE (paternal):
- Shizhé'é - My paternal uncle

SISTER:
- Shidine'é - My older sister
- Shitsilí - My younger sister

BROTHER:
- Shinááí - My older brother
- Shitsilí - My younger brother

COUSIN:
- Shiláán - My cousin (cross-cousin, marriageable)
- Shik'is - My sibling/cousin (parallel cousin, not marriageable)


PART 9: CLAN RELATIONSHIP PHRASES
================================================================================

ASKING SOMEONE'S CLAN:
- "Haash éí binilii?" - What is your clan?
- "Dóoneʼé nishłį́?" - What clan are you?
- "Haash éí nilii?" - What is your clan?

ASKING ABOUT YOUR GRANDMOTHER'S CLAN:
- "Haash éí shimásání binilii?" - What is my grandmother's clan?
- "Shimásání haash nilį́?" - What clan is my grandmother?

EXPLAINING RELATIONSHIPS:
- "Shimásání éí shimá bimá" - My grandmother is my mother's mother
- "Shimásání éí shizhé'é bimá" - My grandmother is my father's mother
- "Shimásání éí shicheii bimá" - My grandmother is my grandfather's mother

SHOWING RESPECT:
- "Shimásání, yá'át'ééh" - Grandmother, hello
- "Shimásání, ahé'hee" - Grandmother, thank you
- "Shimásání, hágoónee'" - Grandmother, goodbye (see you later)


PART 10: WHEN YOU DON'T KNOW SOMEONE'S CLAN
================================================================================

If you don't know someone's clan, use general respectful terms:

For an older woman:
- "Asdzáán" - Woman/Mrs.
- "Amá sání" - Old mother (respectful)

For an older man:
- "Hastiin" - Man/Mr.
- "Azee' sání" - Old medicine man (high respect)

Never use first names for elders unless they specifically invite you to do so.


PART 11: CLANS AND COMMUNITY RESPONSIBILITY
================================================================================

K'é creates responsibilities:

- You must help clan relatives in need
- You are expected to attend clan ceremonies and events
- Your actions reflect on your entire clan
- Shaming yourself brings shame to your clan

The clan system creates a safety net:
- No Diné person is ever homeless without relatives
- Clan connections exist across the entire Navajo Nation
- You can travel anywhere and find clan relatives who will help you


PART 12: CLAN ETIQUETTE SUMMARY
================================================================================

DO:
- Always share your clans when meeting new Diné people
- Learn your four clans (mother, father, maternal grandfather, paternal grandfather)
- Show respect to anyone who shares a clan with you (they are your relative)
- Ask about clan relationships politely

DO NOT:
- Marry within your four clans
- Use first names for elders
- Assume someone's clan based on their last name
- Ignore clan obligations
