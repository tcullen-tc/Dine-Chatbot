NAVAJO TRADITIONAL FOODS

Corn (Naadą́ą́'):
- Most important traditional food
- Eaten as corn mush, cornbread, or roasted
- Blue corn used for ceremonial purposes
- White corn used for everyday meals

Mutton (Dibé atsį'):
- Primary meat source
- Eaten as stew, roasted, or dried
- Sheep herding is central to Navajo economy

Three Sisters (Farming):
- Corn, beans, and squash planted together
- Beans fix nitrogen in soil
- Corn provides structure for beans to climb
- Squash provides ground cover to retain moisture

Pinon Nuts (Neeshch'íí'):
- Harvested in fall (October-November)
- Eaten raw or roasted
- Important traditional food source

Frybread:
- Post-contact food (1860s)
- Made from flour, salt, water, baking powder
- Not traditional but now culturally significant
- Served with mutton, beans, or honey

Wild Greens:
- Tsa' (wild spinach)
- Gathered in spring
- Boiled or added to stews
