================================================================================
DINÉ ETIQUETTE & KINSHIP (K'É)
================================================================================

Based on: Learn in Beauty (NAU), Diné Bizaad Bínáhoo’aah, and Diné Perspectives


PART 1: INTRODUCTION & GREETINGS
================================================================================

In Diné (Navajo) culture, proper introductions are based on k'é (kinship and clan relationships). You never simply say "this is my grandmother" - you establish the relationship through clan connections.

BASIC GREETINGS:
- Yá'át'ééh - Hello / It is good
- Yá'át'ééh abíní - Good morning
- Yá'át'ééh ałní'íní - Good afternoon
- Yá'át'ééh iíłní' - Good evening

RESPECTFUL ADDRESS:
When addressing elders, always use kinship terms, never first names. Showing respect (hochxǫ́ǫ́) is fundamental to Diné culture.


PART 2: FAMILY KINSHIP TERMS
================================================================================

GRANDMOTHER:
- Shimásání - My maternal grandmother
- Shinálí - My paternal grandmother
- Binálí - Her/his paternal grandmother
- Shimásání éí [clan] nílį́ - My grandmother is of [clan]

GRANDFATHER:
- Shimásání (can also be used for maternal grandfather)
- Shinálí (can also be used for paternal grandfather)

MOTHER:
- Shimá - My mother
- Binimá - His/her mother

FATHER:
- Shizhé'é - My father
- Bini' - His/her father

AUNT:
- Shibízhí - My maternal aunt
- Shidá'í - My paternal aunt

UNCLE:
- Shidá'í (can also mean maternal uncle)
- Shizhé'é (can also mean paternal uncle)


PART 3: PROPER INTRODUCTIONS
================================================================================

When introducing yourself or a family member, you share FOUR generations of clans:

First: Your mother's clan (your primary identity)
Second: Your father's clan
Third: Your maternal grandfather's clan
Fourth: Your paternal grandfather's clan

EXAMPLE INTRODUCTION:
"Áshįįhí nishłį́" - I am Salt Clan
"Tódichʼiiʼnii bashishchiin" - Bitter Water Clan is born for me
"Kinyaaʼáanii dashicheii" - Towering House is my maternal grandfather
"Tábąąhá dashinalí" - Water's Edge is my paternal grandfather

INTRODUCING YOUR GRANDMOTHER:
"Shimásání éí Tódichʼiiʼnii nílį́" - My grandmother is of the Bitter Water Clan
"Shimásání éí Kinyaaʼáanii nilį́" - My grandmother is of the Towering House Clan

INTRODUCING YOUR GRANDFATHER:
"Shinálí éí Tábąąhá nílį́" - My grandfather is of the Water's Edge Clan


PART 4: K'É - THE KINSHIP SYSTEM
================================================================================

K'é is the foundational Diné concept of relationships, responsibilities, and belonging.

PRINCIPLES OF K'É:
- K'éí dóó áháyá (Kinship and Respect) - Honoring family and clan relationships
- Áłah nílʼįįh (Cooperation) - Working together for the good of all
- Hodzil (Strength through Unity) - Finding strength in community bonds
- Áłah ałchʼįʼ nahwiildééh (Helping One Another) - Mutual support and care

K'é teaches that:
- You are never alone - you belong to an extended family
- Relationships come with responsibilities
- Clans create kinship bonds across the entire Navajo Nation
- You cannot marry within your own clan or your father's clan


PART 5: PROPER BEHAVIOR WITH ELDERS
================================================================================

RESPECT FOR ELDERS (Ho'áanááhai / K'ééháane'é):
- Always address elders with proper kinship terms
- Do not interrupt when an elder is speaking
- Offer food and drink to elders before serving yourself
- When entering a hogan or home, greet the eldest first
- Never walk between an elder and the fire/stove
- Accept advice and teachings with gratitude

GIVING AND RECEIVING:
- Receive gifts with both hands
- Always offer something in return when receiving help
- Share what you have, especially with family

LISTENING AND LEARNING:
- Diné teachings emphasize listening over speaking
- Learn by observing before asking questions
- Show respect through quiet attention


PART 6: TRADITIONAL VALUES
================================================================================

SA'AH NAAGHÁI BIK'É HÓZHÓ (SNBH) - The Diné Life Path

This is the fundamental Diné philosophy of living a long, healthy, harmonious life:

- Sa'ah (Long Life) - Living with purpose and intention
- Naaghái (Peaceful Life) - Walking in beauty and balance
- Bik'é (Following) - Following the teachings of the Holy People
- Hózhó (Harmony/Balance/Beauty) - The state of being in harmony with all creation

The four elements of Hózhó:
- Nitsáhákees (Thinking) - Clear, positive thoughts
- Nahat'á (Planning) - Living with purpose
- Iiná (Living) - Active, healthy life
- Sihasin (Assurance) - Peace and security

These principles guide all interactions, especially with family and elders.


PART 7: COMMON PHRASES FOR FAMILY SITUATIONS
================================================================================

ASKING HOW SOMEONE IS RELATED:
- "Haash éí binilii?" - What is your clan?
- "Haash éí shimásání binilii?" - What is my grandmother's clan?

EXPLAINING RELATIONSHIPS:
- "Shimásání éí shizhé'é bimá" - My grandmother is my father's mother
- "Shimásání éí shimá bimá" - My grandmother is my mother's mother

SHOWING RESPECT TO GRANDMOTHER:
- "Shimásání, yá'át'ééh" - Grandmother, hello
- "Shimásání, ahé'hee" - Grandmother, thank you
- "Shimásání, hágoónee' - Grandmother, goodbye (see you later)

Sources: Learn in Beauty (NAU), Diné Bizaad Bínáhoo’aah, Diné Perspectives (UA Press), traditional Diné oral teachings.
