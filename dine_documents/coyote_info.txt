Coyote (Ma'ii) is an important trickster figure in Diné (Navajo) mythology and oral traditions.

Coyote is known as a clever, mischievous, and sometimes foolish character who appears in many Diné stories. He often gets into trouble but also helps explain how things came to be in the world.

In one well-known story, Black God was carefully placing the stars in the night sky. While he was working, Coyote grew impatient, grabbed the bag of remaining stars, and scattered them randomly. This is why some stars appear in orderly patterns (where Black God placed them) while others seem scattered across the sky (where Coyote threw them).

Coyote stories are traditionally told during the winter months. These stories teach important lessons about behavior, consequences, and the nature of the world.

As a trickster, Coyote represents both wisdom and foolishness. His actions, whether clever or careless, often reveal deeper truths about human nature and Diné values.

Coyote is also associated with the color gray or blue in the Navajo language - the gray fox is called "màï-çolíj" which means "blue coyote."

Source: Diné oral tradition and Navajo cultural teachings.
