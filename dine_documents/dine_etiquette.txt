In Diné (Navajo) culture, introductions are based on clans (k'é). When introducing yourself or a family member, you share your clan connections.

To introduce your grandmother in Diné, you would say: "Shimásání" (my maternal grandmother) or "Shinálí" (my paternal grandmother).

A full traditional introduction follows this pattern:
- First: Your mother's clan (your primary identity)
- Second: Your father's clan
- Third: Your maternal grandfather's clan  
- Fourth: Your paternal grandfather's clan

For example: "Áshįįhí nishłį́" (I am Salt Clan), "Tódichʼiiʼnii bashishchiin" (Bitter Water Clan is born for me), "Kinyaaʼáanii dashicheii" (Towering House is my maternal grandfather), "Tábąąhá dashinalí" (Water's Edge is my paternal grandfather).

When introducing a grandmother, you would say: "Shimásání éí [her clan] nílį́" (My grandmother is of [her clan]).

Respect for elders is paramount in Diné culture. Always address grandmothers with the proper kinship term rather than by name.

Source: Diné cultural teachings and Navajo language resources.
