Black God (Haashchʼééshzhiní) is one of the most important Holy People in Diné cosmology.

He is known as the creator of the stars. According to Diné tradition, Black God carefully placed the stars in the night sky, arranging them into patterns and constellations. While he was working, Coyote (Ma'ii) grew impatient, grabbed the bag of remaining stars, and scattered them randomly. This is why some stars appear in orderly patterns while others seem scattered.

Black God is also associated with fire and is sometimes called the Fire God. He is a central figure in the Nightway ceremony, a major Diné healing ceremony.

In sandpaintings and ceremonies, Black God is depicted with his face painted black and a white crescent moon on his forehead. The Pleiades star cluster (the Hard Flint Boys) is often shown on his temple or ankle.

Sources: Diné oral tradition, Navajo Religion by Gladys Reichard (1950), Tony Hillerman's research.
