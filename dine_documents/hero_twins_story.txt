# The Hero Twins (Naayéé' Neizghání)

Long ago, after the Diné people emerged into this world, monsters roamed the land, killing and terrorizing the people. The people suffered greatly, and many prayed for help.

Changing Woman, who lived on a mountain in the west, gave birth to twin sons. Their father was the Sun himself. The firstborn was named Naayéé' Neizghání (Monster Slayer), and his younger brother was named Tóbájíshchíní (Born of Water).

When the twins were still young, they asked their mother, "Who is our father?" Changing Woman told them, "Your father is the Sun, who lives in the east." The twins decided they would find him.

They traveled east, facing many dangers along the way. They came to a great turquoise house where Spider Woman lived. She taught them how to protect themselves and gave them feather charms for protection.

Finally, they reached the Sun's house. The Sun was fierce and tested them with many trials—heat, cold, and darkness. The twins passed every test. Impressed, the Sun acknowledged them as his sons and gave them weapons: a knife made of lightning, armor made of sun rays, and a helmet made of black stone.

With these sacred weapons, the twins returned to the world. Monster Slayer asked Spider Woman, "Which monster should we face first?" She told him of a giant named Yé'iitsoh who devoured the Diné people.

Monster Slayer challenged Yé'iitsoh. They fought for four days. On the fourth day, Monster Slayer used his lightning knife to strike down the giant. Where Yé'iitsoh fell, his blood became the source of the red rocks we see today.

The twins continued their journey, defeating all the monsters—the giant birds that carried people away, the bears that attacked without warning, the creatures that caused sickness, and many others. With each victory, the Diné people became safer.

When all the monsters were defeated, the Sun came to his sons and asked, "What do you want?" Monster Slayer replied, "We want our people to live in peace, with the seasons to guide them, and the plants and animals to sustain them."

The Sun blessed the Diné people with corn, squash, and beans—the Three Sisters—and taught them how to farm and live in harmony with the land.

To this day, the Hero Twins are remembered as protectors of the Diné people. Their story teaches us that with courage, perseverance, and the help of family, even the greatest challenges can be overcome.

The eldest twin, Monster Slayer, is sometimes called "Reared Within the Water" in some traditions. His younger brother, Born of Water, is also known as "Child of the Water." Together, they represent the balance of strength and wisdom needed to face life's obstacles.
