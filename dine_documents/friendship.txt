In Diné (Navajo) culture, friendship is deeply connected to the principle of k'é (kinship). K'é extends beyond blood relations to include chosen relationships built on mutual respect, generosity, and responsibility.

Traditional teachings emphasize that a good friend:
- Shows kindness and respect (hózhó)
- Helps without expecting something in return
- Speaks positively about others
- Is trustworthy and keeps confidences

The clan system teaches that all relationships, including friendships, should be approached with the same care as family relationships. Making friends involves being a good relative - showing up, helping, and treating others with the respect you would show your own family.
