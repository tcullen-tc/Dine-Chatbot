In Diné (Navajo) culture, marriage is a sacred bond that extends beyond two individuals—it connects families, clans, and communities. The traditional Navajo wedding ceremony (called "Iina" or "life") is a beautiful expression of hózhó (harmony/balance).

Key principles for a good marriage in Diné tradition include:

- K'é (kinship): Marriage joins two families, not just two people. The couple's responsibility extends to caring for both families.
- Hózhó (balance/harmony): A good marriage requires balance—listening, patience, and working through challenges together.
- Respect: Traditional teachings emphasize that partners should treat each other with the same respect they show to elders.
- Communication: The Navajo way encourages speaking kindly, avoiding harsh words, and resolving conflicts with calmness.
- Shared responsibility: Couples work together to provide for the family and participate in community life.

Traditional Navajo weddings involve a ceremony with corn pollen, a blessing from a medicine person, and a symbolic exchange of water, representing life and continuity. The couple pledges to live in harmony, to support each other, and to raise children with Diné values.
