Tsiiyéél (pronounced "see-yell") is the traditional Navajo hair bun worn by women.

The Tsiiyéél is made by wrapping long hair with yarn, typically white or black wool yarn, into a bun shape. It is often decorated with white shells, turquoise, or silver.

The hair bun is not just a hairstyle - it carries deep cultural significance. It represents the sacred number four (the four directions, four seasons), and the wrapping of the hair is said to represent the wrapping of the earth.

Traditionally, unmarried women wear the Tsiiyéél at the back of the head, while married women wear it on the side. The style can indicate a woman's clan, marital status, and community role.

The Tsiiyéél is still worn today during ceremonies, weddings, and special events as a way to honor Diné traditions.
