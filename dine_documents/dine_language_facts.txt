================================================================================
DINÉ LANGUAGE FACTS (Navajo Language)
================================================================================
Source: Learn in Beauty (NAU), Navajo Nation Government, Ethnologue


PART 1: SPEAKER STATISTICS
================================================================================

Current number of Navajo speakers: Approximately 148,530

Total Navajo population: 250,000 - 275,000

Percentage of Navajo people who speak the language: ~54%

Language vitality status: Endangered (UNESCO classification)

Navajo is the most widely spoken Native American language in the United States.


PART 2: WHY THE LANGUAGE IS ENDANGERED
================================================================================

Historical factors:
- Boarding school era (late 1800s - 1960s) where Navajo children were punished for speaking their language
- English-only education policies
- Economic pressures to learn English
- Urbanization and migration away from Navajo Nation

Modern factors:
- Fewer parents teaching Navajo to their children
- Dominance of English in media and technology
- Limited Navajo language resources in schools


PART 3: PRESERVATION EFFORTS
================================================================================

Navajo Nation initiatives:
- Navajo Language revitalization programs
- Diné Bizaad curriculum in Navajo Nation schools
- Navajo language immersion schools (Tuba City, Window Rock, others)
- Navajo Language Academy teacher training

Educational resources:
- Diné Bizaad Bínáhoo’aah (Rediscovering the Navajo Language)
- Rosetta Stone Navajo edition
- Navajo language apps and online dictionaries

Community efforts:
- Navajo language radio stations (KTNN, others)
- Navajo language social media groups
- Navajo Word of the Day programs


PART 4: UNIQUE FEATURES OF DINÉ BIZAAD
================================================================================

Navajo is an Athabaskan language, related to Apache languages.

Unique characteristics:
- Mostly verbs - verbs carry most of the meaning
- Four tones (high, low, rising, falling)
- Complex verb system with up to 500 prefixes
- No adjectives - descriptive words are verbs
- Word order: Subject-Object-Verb (different from English Subject-Verb-Object)

Example of verb complexity:
- "Naashá" - I am going
- "Yishááł" - I am going (future)
- "Neesháázh" - I went (past)
- "Deeshááł" - I am about to go


PART 5: COMMON DINÉ BIZAAD PHRASES
================================================================================

Greetings:
- Yá'át'ééh - Hello / It is good
- Yá'át'ééh abíní - Good morning
- Yá'át'ééh ałní'íní - Good afternoon
- Yá'át'ééh iíłní' - Good evening
- Hágoónee' - Goodbye (see you later)

Polite phrases:
- Ahé'hee - Thank you
- T'áá shǫǫdí - You're welcome
- Nizhóní - It's beautiful / good
- Hózhǫ́ - Harmony / peace / beauty
- Dooda - No
- Aoo' - Yes

Family terms:
- Shimá - My mother
- Shizhé'é - My father
- Shimásání - My maternal grandmother
- Shinálí - My paternal grandmother
- Shicheii - My maternal grandfather
- Shinálí - My paternal grandfather
- Shibízhí - My maternal aunt
- Shidá'í - My paternal aunt
